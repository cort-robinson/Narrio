# Narrio architecture

Follow-along text adds `GutenbergTextDiscovery`, `BookTextParser`, and `FollowAlongStore` beside the recording and delivery providers. Text retains its own content identity and locators; timing choices belong to a specific audio source/part and use the existing playback clock. Schema 5 retains multiple ebook editions and adds a sequence-guarded shared position without replacing listening history. See [follow-along logistics](FOLLOW_ALONG.md) for current boundaries and the planned shared cursor for a full reader.

## Book before recording, recording before URL

`Audiobook` can represent a metadata-only book (`provider = catalog`) or a narrated recording. Catalog IDs hash normalized title/author identity and are independent of metadata-provider edition IDs. Catalog entries have no audio source, torrent, or checked availability. Selecting a listening source uses that recording's original ID; saved progress remains tied to recording/source/part. Internet Archive item IDs distinguish recordings, even when titles repeat. Narrator and language are read from source metadata; missing details stay visibly unknown. `AudioSource` represents a particular delivery and file layout. `AudioPart` has a stable ID, filename, optional duration, and Archive URL or TorBox torrent/file IDs. Generated CDN links never become source identity.

`BookCatalog` owns initial identification through Apple Books (charts for browsing, audiobook search first for queries), Audible, Google Books, and Open Library. `RecordingDiscovery` is invoked separately by `BookSourceDiscovery` from book details. `ArchiveDiscovery` uses Internet Archive advanced search and item metadata. Recording results need usable audio files. MP3 derivatives at reduced bitrate are excluded when matching original audio is available; numeric ordering includes disc paths. M4B remains a separate layout. Samples, trailers, and previews are excluded from Archive and TorBox audio layouts.

`DeliveryProvider` owns preparation, status, source files, and near-playback resolution. `TorBoxDelivery` uses the current TorBox Main API through a direct authenticated connection. It reuses an account torrent matching the recording's hash, or uploads the recording's actual `.torrent` file. A torrent URL is not passed as a magnet. Availability requires both `download_finished` and `download_present`. Preparation remains in Room so the activity can leave and return. The app polls status while that recording's detail is visible and also exposes a manual check.

`KnabenDiscovery` adds actual audiobook release search using the public v2 API and category 1003000. Torrent hashes distinguish releases. Original release titles and filenames stay visible when catalog metadata supplies a cleaner display title. Unknown recording language and abridgment remain unverified. Zero-seeder releases are included because they may still be cached. Queries contain no TorBox credential. TorBox library search remains explicit account-library browsing.

## Book metadata

Browsing without a query uses `AppleBooks`: the public Apple audiobook top chart for the selected category (store-wide for All), then one batched iTunes lookup for descriptions and artwork. Apple pairs thrillers with mysteries in one audiobook genre and has no horror audiobook genre, so Horror uses Apple's paid horror ebook chart and ebook details; those are widely published books. Store-wide charts reflect books sold across stores, so store exclusives such as Audible Originals and the indie box sets that keyword browsing surfaced are absent; those had few or no listening sources. Preorders and dramatized or multi-part adaptations are excluded, releases from the last 14 days follow established titles in chart order, and store titles lose series, award, and marketing labels ("A Novel", "Book 1", book club picks) while real subtitles remain. Chart pages are cached for six hours. If the charts are unavailable or too short, browsing falls back to keyword catalog search without Audible Originals and retries the charts after five minutes. Typed searches use Apple's audiobook search: search words must appear in the store title (which keeps the series name) or author, and translated recordings, identified by a translator credit, an edition label, or a foreign series label, are skipped. When Apple returns fewer than eight books or is unavailable, the Audible catalog fills the gaps, including store exclusives; an Apple outage is retried after five minutes.

Audible's own productions carry an "Only from Audible" banner on their artwork in every store, Apple included. Audible-published books (Audible's `publisher_name`, Apple's copyright line) therefore take a plain cover from another edition, or else from the same book's Apple ebook: one author-term ebook search covers a whole series, for up to four authors per list. Ebook matches skip translations (edition labels and non-English ISBN artwork) and ebook listings that reuse the audiobook art. Without a plain cover (Audible Originals), the banner art stays. Cover results are remembered for the session; a failed lookup shortens the list's cache to five minutes.

`BookCatalog` retrieves author, description, and artwork before publishing search results. Apple Books is primary and Audible fills gaps; Google Books fills sparse/missing results, and Open Library search plus up to six paced work lookups is the final fallback. Providers receive book queries without account credentials. Unrelated keyword recommendations are excluded. Normalized title/author identity collapses catalog editions while preserving different authors and sequels. Complete descriptions/covers are preferred over sparse entries; when none are complete, remaining real metadata stays visible rather than fabricated. Multiple catalog narrator editions remain unspecified. A 32-query memory cache retains successful lists for a day and empty results for five minutes; outages remain retryable and requests support cancellation.

`BookMetadata` enriches existing Knaben and TorBox releases through the public Audible catalog, falling back to Open Library search and work details. It retrieves full publisher descriptions, authors, named narrators, and the largest secure cover image. Search excludes podcasts and requires an exact normalized title or a matching title/author release name; conflicting authors, summaries, collections, and unrelated titles are rejected. Reversed author names and bilingual release names are supported. Existing source narrators are retained. When several catalog recordings have different narrators, a matching narrator in the original release name is required; otherwise narration stays unknown. Catalog narrators are always labeled as catalog information, and do not verify the downloaded recording's edition.

Internet Archive retains recording-specific metadata, prioritizing explicit narrator/reader fields and original front/cover artwork over thumbnail derivatives. Shared plain-text cleanup preserves initials, paragraphs, and named/numeric HTML entities. Catalog covers render without cropping or duplicate title overlays; the original Narrio art remains visible while loading and after image errors.

Initial book results already contain metadata and do not request recordings or TorBox. Existing recording detail enrichment has its own loading state and never disables listening. Each metadata request has an eight-second deadline, cancellation closes pending requests, and at most two recording-enrichment lookups run concurrently. A bounded 128-entry enrichment cache retains successful responses for a day and empty results for five minutes; failed requests remain retryable. Shelf books store descriptive metadata and its retrieval time in backward-compatible book JSON. Transactional updates copy only book details, preserving stored recording/source IDs, files, cache state, source histories, preparation state, and listening positions. Explicit refresh bypasses successful-cache reuse. No database schema migration is needed.

Metadata requests contain only cleaned release/book names. They never include the TorBox key, file lists, temporary playback URLs, or listening history. Metadata outages preserve existing details and never change audio availability.

Collection names remain candidates until file checks finish. A collection needs an exact author segment or verified author metadata, plus a file/folder matching the requested book. `SourceQuality` projects only those book files and assigns a book-specific recording ID, keeping different books in one torrent separate on the shelf and in playback history. Wrong volume numbers and summary/preview releases remain excluded; an ambiguous collection is never offered as a whole book.

A release whose name matches the book can still hold a whole series, because a series often shares its first book's name ("Dungeon Crawler Carl"). After matching, `SourceQuality.oneBook` checks whether each source's files divide into books: a folder per book, files numbered "Book N", or several differently named M4B files. It keeps the group that names this title with the least other wording, giving the same book-specific ID. Pieces of one book (CD 1/CD 2, Part 1/Part 2, chapter files) stay whole. Numbered books that don't name this title, or two equally good groups, aren't chosen automatically.

Once a collection has been narrowed to a book, its persisted `bookFilesSelected` flag makes TorBox cache and ready-file refreshes retain those formats and filenames. A different format belonging to a sibling book cannot enter the selected recording during preparation or playback. Ordinary single-book recordings can still discover additional formats for that book.

`BookSourceDiscovery` runs automatically when a catalog book opens, querying public recordings, every enabled source add-on, and account audio; results are reused for ten minutes per book and connection state. `SourceQuality.confidence` rates each release: STRONG needs one release-name segment naming exactly this title plus author evidence (full name, first and last name, or initials); POSSIBLE has the title without author evidence, or a `Title: words` name the catalog subtitle doesn't confirm. Summaries, collections, ranges such as `Books 1-6`, other series books, and partial uploads such as `(4 of 5)` are rejected. Only verified STRONG recordings are chosen automatically; POSSIBLE and unverified releases are listed for manual review. Ranking prefers ready audio, the book's language, unabridged readings, whole-book files, and seeders. `SourceQuality.edition` labels narrator, dramatization/abridgment, and language from provider metadata or release names, and `versions` offers a choice only when those differ or a public LibriVox reading exists beside a release. Torrent metadata for uncached releases is read only when no ready recording was found. Public recording hydration is capped at twelve recordings in batches of four; the full response is matched again. Indexed files are checked through `torrents/checkcached?format=object&list_files=true`; failed checks do not fall back to unchecked releases. Results need usable audio and either direct public HTTPS delivery, checked cached audio, or verified uncached files with reported seeders. `TorrentFileDiscovery` reads public iTorrents metadata after a successful cache miss, capped at eight strongest releases, in parallel, with twelve-second request deadlines. It checks the original bencoded info hash, bounds payload size/nesting/entries, validates file paths, excludes samples and non-audio files, and rechecks title/author against the manifest name. This reads file metadata only; discovery never joins peers, fetches audio, or creates account downloads. Indexed seeder counts are availability evidence, not a guarantee of preparation success. Uncached results are labelled and expose only explicit preparation until ready; default streaming and phone downloads remain disabled. A partial file cache cannot mark a verified whole recording ready. Torrent hashes deduplicate account/index hits, retaining distinct recordings and layouts. Cached sources appear first; partial failures remain visible beside successful results. Bitrate is not invented from a filename or format. Source selection copies work description/artwork while preserving recording ID, narrator, language, original release title, files, and availability. The default new-source playback path checks cache availability before source creation. Completion still requires both readiness flags. Cache snapshots can change; live authenticated TorBox verification remains separate.

## Playback

`ListeningService` owns ExoPlayer, a MediaSession, playlist, speed, timer, and progress persistence. UI actions address the local service; the media session accepts the app and trusted system controllers, with playlist/URI mutation commands removed. Android handles audio focus, becoming-noisy events, foreground media notification, and network wake mode.

Playlist URIs are `narrio://audio/<stable-part-id>`. `RefreshingDataSource` resolves each part only when opened. TorBox links live in a concurrent in-memory cache. HTTP 401/403/404/410 triggers a fresh link and retries the same Media3 `DataSpec`, preserving the byte offset. A visible playback recovery action also renews links and retries from the player's current position. Provider JSON errors use generic messages instead of echoing responses that could contain secrets.

Catalog/API calls have bounded deadlines. Audio requests have no whole-request deadline, because a valid long stream can exceed it; connection/read timeouts still apply. Media3 buffers 30–90 seconds, starts after a short buffer, and re-buffers on network interruption. Nero `chpl` metadata is read using bounded HTTP ranges; ID3 chapter frames are accepted from Media3. Unsupported metadata falls back to ordered parts.

## Persistence

Room has `shelf`, `positions`, `bookmarks`, `book_text`, `text_bindings`, `ebook_editions`, `shared_positions`, `annotations`, and `alignment_jobs` tables. Position identity is `(recordingId, sourceId, stablePartId)`, with milliseconds inside that part. Source-format changes have independent histories, so an MP3 timestamp is never copied to M4B. Restoring a source locates the stable part in its current ordered list. Bookmarks retain their source ID and can restore an earlier layout. Progress is saved periodically while playing and on relevant player events. Reopening the app reconstructs the last playlist paused; audio preparation waits for an explicit resume.

The database is schema 5. Migration 1→2 adds per-source histories; 2→3 adds the pending audio format; 3→4 adds text attachments and source/part-specific chapter choices and timing anchors. Migration 4 to 5 retains existing attachments as active editions, copies anchor JSON unchanged into edition-scoped bindings, adds optional text cursors to bookmarks, and seeds the shared position from the shelf's listening source/part. Edition originals and normalized JSON retain their existing paths; available document metadata is read locally during migration, and missing files never block the listening-library upgrade. Old-player progress cannot overwrite an independently preparing source, and choosing M4B survives reopening the recording. Removing a shelf item clears its histories, bookmarks, attached text, and phone downloads, and its Advanced sourcing choices (hidden releases, search words, file choices) and phone-file read access; it does not delete provider files. Those choices live in the private `advanced-sources` preferences, not Room, and file choices apply at playback without changing source or part IDs (see [fixing sources yourself](SOURCE_SEARCH.md#fixing-sources-yourself-advanced)). Phone audio is a `local` delivery: playlist parts resolve to their document URI and play through Android's content data source. Removing only book text preserves listening history and bookmarks. Annotations and alignment jobs belong to an edition and cascade when it is removed.

`AppGraph.sharedPositions` implements the shared `SharedPositionStore` contract. Room commits check `basedOnSequence` and increment it in one transaction; absence has sequence zero, and stale writers receive null. Per-source audio history remains independent; activity thresholds and mapping are workstream C's responsibility. `AppGraph.editionFiles` implements `EditionFiles` through `FollowAlongStore`. Edition activation changes only `book_text`; switching back restores that edition's anchors. Removing the active edition chooses the oldest remaining edition. Private originals and normalized JSON use fingerprint-only paths, bounded parsing, serialized mutations, synced temporary writes, and atomic renames.

`LocalEbookImporter.import(uri)` accepts EPUB/TXT, reads OPF title/author/language (TXT falls back to the filename), and creates a source-free shelf book keyed by normalized title/author. It attaches the edition before best-effort catalog enrichment, so offline import remains usable. Reimporting another edition of the same title/author reuses the book and preserves its audio. It never creates or prepares an audio source.

For the other reader workstreams, `LibraryDao.observeShelfState(audio, ebook)` supplies format flags, shared position, last mode, and nullable progression; both arguments true selects Both. Unknown narration durations return unknown progression. `NarrioViewModel.shelfState`, `editions(bookId)`, `sharedPosition(bookId)`, and the suspend import/activate/remove methods expose this data without new screens. Annotations store both serialized `ContentCursor` endpoints, selected text, color, note, and timestamps. Alignment rows are keyed by book/edition/recording/source/part and retain status, progress, resume time, content/audio fingerprints, and normalization/alignment versions. `BookmarkEntry.contentCursor()` restores optional text locators while audio bookmark columns remain unchanged. Shared contract types in `Reading.kt` are unchanged.

## Appearance

`AppearanceSettings` keeps mode, palette, font, text size, and one optional named custom palette independent. `AppearanceStore` saves normalized JSON under the local SharedPreferences key `appearance.v1`. Its codec ignores future optional fields, coerces unknown enum values to defaults, and falls back safely on damaged data. Reading can migrate the legacy `theme` mode; the next save removes that old key while preserving unrelated preferences. Appearance requires no provider, account, font download, or network request.

The ViewModel publishes saved settings through a StateFlow. `NarrioApp` passes them to `NarrioTheme`, which resolves Night/Day/System mode, then supplies app-wide Material color and typography roles. Listening room retains the authored original schemes. Ocean, Forest, Rosewood, Graphite, and Custom use separate Night/Day accent, supporting, and background seeds; the generator derives tonal surfaces and readable foreground/action/container roles, with minimum ratios of 4.5 for content and 3.0 for the `outline` role. Tertiary follows secondary. System-bar icon choice uses foreground contrast against the actual scheme background. Font choices reuse bundled Newsreader/Manrope or Android's default family; app text-size multipliers remain in sp so system scaling is retained.

Preset, mode, font, and text-size selections save immediately. The custom editor holds a `rememberSaveable` draft and renders it inside a contained `NarrioTheme` preview. Only **Save & use theme** publishes and persists that draft; cancellation leaves the applied settings intact. Preset switching and default reset retain the saved custom palette.

## Offline audio

Media3 `DownloadService`, `DownloadManager`, a durable `DownloadIndex`, and `SimpleCache` handle explicit phone downloads. The cache lives in private app files with `NoOpCacheEvictor`; the user removes audio explicitly. Requests persist only stable audio URIs and recording/source metadata. Their resolving data source renews TorBox links near transfer time, including after process recreation. Downloads run with a data-sync foreground service and network-constrained scheduling, at most two transfers in parallel. Wi-Fi/unmetered is the default. Pause/resume uses persisted stop reasons; foreground timeouts pause downloads so they can be resumed explicitly.

Playback wraps the refreshing network source in a **read-only** `CacheDataSource`: its cache-write sink is null. Streaming therefore does not populate the offline cache. Explicit completed downloads can play and seek without an account connection or network. M4B chapter reading tries bounded cached head/tail data for downloaded files. Disconnect preserves completed audio but pauses unfinished TorBox downloads and clears in-memory links. UI download progress polls only while the activity is visible; Media3 owns transfers and their foreground notification independently.

## Fold and window state

Compose uses safe drawing/navigation insets, live window constraints, and WindowManager `FoldingFeature`. Compact windows show one destination. Expanded windows show a rail and two panes. A separating vertical hinge defines the pane boundary; a half-open horizontal hinge divides the full player into artwork and controls. Search, selection, and destination live in the ViewModel; playback lives in the service. Window resizing therefore changes presentation without changing the recording/session.

## Credentials and release

Android Keystore AES/GCM protects the TorBox key. SharedPreferences stores ciphertext and an IV only. The app rejects cleartext traffic, disables backup/device transfer of its local files, and has no network logger or analytics. Disconnect pauses online TorBox playback, clears temporary links, deletes the stored key, and removes its Keystore alias. Completed phone downloads continue to work.

The supplied release is R8 optimized and locally signed. The ignored signing identity is retained for subsequent app updates. The repository contains no TorBox account credential.

## Reader

The ebook reader (`app.narrio.reader`) renders EPUB with the Readium Kotlin Toolkit; plain text is converted once by `TxtEpub` into a minimal EPUB in the cache and shown by the same reader. `ReaderBook` opens an edition with network access refused and wraps Readium's container so every resource passes through `ReaderDocuments` before the navigator's WebView sees it: `EpubSanitizer` removes publication scripts, event handlers, script URLs, frames, plugins, and remote references in markup, SVG, and CSS. Readium injects its own scripts afterwards (`WebViewServer` serves `publication.get()` output, then `injectHtml`), so they keep working. A hostile fixture EPUB proves the filter in JVM and emulator tests.

Positions use the follow-along `(resource, offset)` space. The served XHTML is parsed as XML (faithful to the WebView); its blocks are aligned with `BookTextParser`'s HTML parse and marked with `data-narrio-o` offsets, and a reproduction of jsoup 1.21.2 `Element.text()` maps raw page characters to parser offsets. jsoup is pinned at 1.21.2 because 1.23 changes `text()` separators and would shift stored offsets. A page probe reports the first visible character; `CursorMapping` turns it into a `ContentCursor` and back into a Readium text-quote locator that lands on the same character. Plain-text EPUB files carry the parser's `"text"` offsets directly.

`ReaderController` is the reusable component (cursor, visible range, programmatic moves, decorations and selection by content range, follow hooks for together mode). Its cursor changes only with the reader's own navigation; rotation, folding, font, and layout changes restore it. `ReaderSession` commits reading through `SharedPositionStore` after a 3 s dwell or the second page turn, never on open, and not while the same book's audio plays, and keeps a reading pace on the device for "minutes left in chapter". `EditionFiles` uses durable multi-edition originals from `FollowAlongStore`; shared positions use one ownership-gated Room store, surviving process restarts.

Read along (`ui/ReadAlong.kt`) runs on top of the same controller. `Narration` (pure) maps the media clock of the playing part to a parser passage, and to a word only where the place is EXACT; the clock advances between service publications at the playback speed. Marks are `NarrationMark` decoration styles registered with the navigator (a blended sentence wash, a dotted estimate underline, a word mark), redrawn once each resource is laid out. `follow` keeps the mapped cursor on screen and is the place relayouts restore while following; explicit page turns end the navigator's settling window so they always stop following. With `textTaps` on, taps on text report the character under the finger (`ReaderScripts.at`) and play from its sentence through `seekFromText`. `ReadAlongLayout` places the page and controls as a tray, a side panel (or the pane beyond a separating vertical hinge), or the tabletop halves, keeping the page as the first child so the navigator is never recreated by turning read along on or off. Entering from a page commits it explicitly (`ReaderSession.commitNow`) and starts the recording there; leaving for the Listening room realigns audio to a newer reading place (`ListeningService.alignToSharedPosition`). Both offer Undo for large moves.

`MainActivity` is a `FragmentActivity` so Compose can host the navigator fragment; navigator fragments restored after recreation are replaced at the saved cursor. Readium 3.4 requires AGP 9.1+, compileSdk 37, core library desugaring, and its AndroidX (lifecycle 2.11, Compose 1.11) and media3 1.11 lines.
